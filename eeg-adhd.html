EEG-Based ADHD Classification

Machine Learning for Objective Neurodiagnostics Using Traditional ML Models

Project Type

Academic Research

Domain

Neuroscience & Machine Learning

Duration

4 months

Project Overview

ADHD is typically diagnosed through subjective behavioral assessments — a process prone to inconsistency and bias. I led a machine learning project to classify ADHD vs. neurotypical individuals using EEG brain signals, with the goal of making diagnosis more objective and data-driven.

What I Built

I led a team of three to process EEG recordings from children aged 7–12 (19 channels, 128 Hz) using bandpass filtering, ICA artifact removal, and 2-second epoching. From the cleaned signals, I extracted three neurophysiologically grounded features — Theta/Beta Ratio, Alpha Power, and Slow Wave Activity — and trained SVM, Random Forest, and KNN classifiers on each.

Results

The best model — SVM on Theta/Beta Ratio features — hit 80.6% accuracy, well above chance and approaching clinically useful thresholds. SVM also proved the most consistent across all three feature types, while frontal and central channels emerged as the strongest predictors, which aligns with what neuroscience tells us about ADHD.

Impact & Next Steps

This work demonstrates that traditional ML, paired with the right biomarkers, can meaningfully support ADHD screening. Going forward, I plan to explore connectivity features and CNN/LSTM architectures to push performance further.

Immediate Next Steps

  • Feature Enhancement: Explore connectivity measures, nonlinear features, and time-frequency analysis for improved discrimination
  • Deep Learning Integration: Compare traditional models with CNN, LSTM, and EEGNet architectures for potential performance gains
  • Dataset Expansion: Address current limitations in dataset size and demographic diversity
